*AMBUDA DCS*

This was originally developed by Oliver Hellwig. It is a massive corpus of over 600,000 sentences (and millions of tokens) that were semi-automatically annotated over several years.

Ambuda has a growing network of volunteers - ranging from students to expert scholars - who use the platform to proofread and correct the machine-generated parses.
Vidyut Engine: Ambuda uses its own open-source software, Vidyut (specifically vidyut-cheda), to provide initial “guesses” for word splitting and morphology. Volunteers then review these guesses to ensure accuracy.
Volunteers must first identify where sandhi (phonetic joining) has occurred and split the string into its base words.
Each word is tagged with specific attributes:
Subanta (Nouns/Adjectives): Gender (Linga), Case (Vibhakti), and Number (Vacana).
Tiṅanta (Verbs): Person (Purusha), Number (Vacana), Tense/Mood (Lakara), and Voice (Prayoga).

All this data is provided on their github at https://github.com/ambuda-org/dcs
The parsed data is included on our github itself.
